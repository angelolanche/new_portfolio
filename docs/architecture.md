# Architecture and conventions

Use Vite, React, strict TypeScript, Three.js, React Three Fiber, and Node.js tooling. Use npm and maintain package-lock.json with dependency changes. Store content locally; a backend, CMS, remote storage, and additional languages are future decisions. GSAP, shaders, and GLSL are options when justified, not required additions.

Organize by feature and create directories only when needed:

```text
src/app/                 App setup, routing, global styles
src/features/world/      3D scene, camera, navigation
src/features/projects/   Project presentation and detail pages
src/features/about/      About presentation
src/features/contact/    Contact presentation
src/components/          Shared HTML components
src/content/             Typed shared content
src/assets/              Imported assets
public/                  Directly served assets
```

Share content between the 3D experience and HTML fallback. Keep content separate from presentation and define its shape with TypeScript types. Use PascalCase for React components and camelCase for functions and variables. Keep modules focused; add abstractions when actual needs justify them. Follow existing patterns and use the configured Prettier and ESLint tools.

## World source map

Read the module for the requested behavior first. `Opening.tsx` is the HTML entry point, not the scene implementation. World filenames below are relative to `src/features/world/`.

Group modules by responsibility: `opening/` owns the canvas and camera; `room/` owns scene assembly and layout, with objects in `room/details/` and lighting in `room/lighting/`; `surfaces/` owns shared materials, geometry and static batching; `exterior/` owns the photographic backdrop, porch and window trim; `dither/` owns post-processing and its shader; `signal-world/` owns the CRT signal and universe. Keep the water simulation in `cup-spill/` and tests beside their modules. Only the HTML entry point stays at the world root.

Within `signal-world/`, `rendering/` owns CRT compositing and entry, `navigation/` owns travel/free flight/look, `composition/` owns forms/threads/sun, `media/` owns the video works and title, and `plaques/` owns labels/materials/placement. Shared layout and float sampling stay at its root. The physical television remains in `room/TelevisionCabinet.tsx` and `room/details/TelevisionHardware.tsx`.

Read `Opening.tsx` only for the HTML shell, loading, dialogs and History API URLs. Keyboard and wheel gestures live in `opening/useOpeningControls.ts`; mouse/touch handlers live in `opening/useOpeningPointer.ts`. Regression suites in `opening/tests/` are split into `Opening.navigation.test.tsx`, `Opening.look.test.tsx`, `Opening.playback.test.tsx`, `Opening.routes.test.tsx` and `Opening.touch.test.tsx`, with shared canvas/dialog setup in `openingHarness.tsx`. Search test names before reading full suites. Keep the connected CRT lifecycle test in `signal-world/rendering/TelevisionStatic.test.tsx` for handoff/camera/render-state changes; do not load it for unrelated room or UI work.

`signal-world/media/SignalTitle.tsx` uses the supplied `references/aosol-efeito.mp4` as a local video texture on a thin white 3D block, preserving the exact lettering and chromatic loop. `SignalPlaques.tsx` renders raycastable 3D blocks with normal depth/frustum testing. `plaqueText.ts` rasterizes titles and pure-black metadata without numbering; `plaqueMaterial.ts` animates only the title with the shared clock and no new decoders. On each completed scroll/Locate journey, `plaqueLayout.ts` fits only the destination label below or beside its film, reducing scale as needed. `plaquePlacement.ts` considers visible film bounds and aligns candidates inside the arrival viewport; above-film and viewport-docked placements are excluded. Looking around, flying, changing mode, resizing or opening/closing a dialog cannot refit saved labels. No distance cutoff hides labels, and no camera-following overlay or special click priority remains. ## Performance contract

- Movement starts after two completed room/CRT frames, discarding their delta so initial GPU setup cannot consume the camera path. CRT composition uses a separate scene containing only the screen plane. Water meshes keep current bounds for frustum culling; the tabletop uploads only active buffer prefixes and clears only active normals. Tests cover bounds and transfer volume (under 10% of reserved capacity at the sampled spill times); this is not an FPS measurement.

- The HTML entry loads independently of `opening/OpeningCanvas.tsx`. Lazy loading makes the shell available sooner; it does not eliminate the cost of downloading, constructing or compiling the 3D scene.
- Startup shows a small, rough black-and-white TV sketch from the initial HTML beneath an oversized red favicon sun. Three separate rope passes cross its front; connecting loops turn around the sides behind the TV, and the slack end disappears behind the sun. The supplied rough TV-and-rope sketch is served as a 51 KB JPEG with CSS inversion; a screen mask keeps progress beneath the rope. A monochrome static texture fills the screen from bottom to top as preparation progresses. The tether has white contours and twist marks around a black interior. “loading...” sits below the illustration with red/cyan separation and brief RGB offsets; reduced motion disables its flicker. The initial HTML preloads the three illustration files and reveals their artwork together after decoding. The React overlay keeps the first overlay visible until its own images decode, avoiding a blank or partially drawn handoff; it keeps the same markup and accessible progressbar. `opening/scenePreload.ts` warms the exterior/title posters and both video decoders in parallel with the canvas module. The exterior MP4 has its `moov` atom before `mdat`, so playback can begin from downloaded initial data. Progress reflects completed preparation tasks, rather than elapsed time or downloaded bytes. The overlay has an 800 ms minimum, using one start time across the Suspense/canvas transition. Preloading has a 10-second deadline, but a slow exterior video continues to hold the opening until its first decoded frame renders; the poster releases the gate only after a video error, for reduced motion, or for direct signal URLs where the exterior is invisible. Warm-up frames start counting after that signal and all scene assets resolve. The overlay then reaches 100%, holds briefly and fades over 600 ms; camera playback stays paused through the fade. This keeps the animated exterior visible from the first opening frame without claiming a measured loading-time or FPS gain.
- `CameraMove` runs at frame priority -1, then room animations update, then `ColorDither` renders at priority 1, then `TelevisionStatic` composes the CRT at priority 2. At 11.4 seconds, the TV renderer owns the viewport and room post-processing is skipped.
- The room materials receive a stable context value and its memoized composition does not rebuild when dialogs suspend animation.
- Camera curve constants are computed per viewport aspect. Cached poses still restore the base orientation every frame before pointer damping, including during loading and inside the TV.
- Room-only animation work stops when covered by the TV. Water uses a forward-only fixed-step deterministic simulation. `cup-spill/spillVisibility.ts` computes conservative current-state envelopes for each water part using the actual camera and table transform. Invisible parts skip mesh rebuilding; physics continues and looking back refreshes newly visible parts. Detached droplets are no longer rendered; their conserved emission data still drives the edge sheet. Thin water uses the room's baked reflection with a standard transparent material; refraction remains inside the cup and on the glass rim. The renderer's transmission target uses 75% linear resolution, independent of canvas DPR.
- Static batching includes opaque standard detail materials as well as RoomMaterial, comparing their complete appearance, texture identities, layers, geometry layouts and render order. Batches use 1.5 m regions to retain conservative frustum culling. Hidden/explicitly excluded subtrees, physical/transparent materials, custom callbacks and animated geometry stay separate; animated furniture, water and lights remain outside the static subtree. Mirrored transforms reverse baked triangle winding, retaining correct front faces and normals on shoes. Closed FinishedBox/Upholstery models and the closed shoe body use FrontSide; open ankle fabric, palhinha and other thin surfaces retain DoubleSide. Reflection quality/intensity is unchanged: reducing intensity alone would not remove its shader work. Cleanup restores sources and disposes merged geometry. During development, hard-reload after changing room openings or furniture transforms: Vite hot updates can leave the mount-time merged geometry visible alongside updated source objects. Verify room visuals after a fresh mount.
- The curved media backdrop must remain outside the veranda and cover both apertures throughout the camera path. `exterior/exteriorLayout.test.ts` checks actual ray intersections, UV edge margins and porch sightlines across landscape, portrait and extreme aspect ratios. The former globe, rings, particles and procedural nebula are removed.
- Preserve pixel ratio, segment counts, procedural textures, shadows, room reflections, the simplified water treatment above, dither, signal cadence and all approved timings. The continuous render loop remains necessary for the live TV signal and damped pointer look; loading and dialogs suspend animation time. There are no user pause or replay controls.

Regression checks cover camera continuity/framing, dialog suspension, render handoff, geometry bounds/cleanup, exterior visibility and deterministic water. Real-device FPS, cold/warm loading time, GPU memory and visual equivalence require browser/device measurements; bundle size alone is not a loading-time measurement.

The elevated sun uses a merged sphere and needle corona from `signalSunGeometry.ts`, based on the supplied reference in `references/sun-reference.png`; the final stop climbs and frames the sun. `SignalSun.tsx` applies the independent procedural plasma and atmosphere shaders in `signalSunShader.ts`, using the shared world clock so animations freeze with the scene. SunInstructions places white WASD and arrow key clusters below and beside the sun, oriented to the final stop, with English guidance to the left of each keyboard. The WASD cluster sits left of the sun and the arrow cluster lower on the right; desktop scale is 20% larger and scales down for narrow viewports. Each keyboard shares a float phase to preserve key spacing. Lettering occupies the box front material directly, without a nearly coplanar overlay that can flicker. Solar detail fades below pixel resolution to limit distant shimmer. The red/crimson plasma evolves at 0.32 clock units per second; shared time also drives corona displacement and halo pulses. The geometry bounds include displacement padding. Thread exclusions use a 65-unit `signalSunClearanceRadius` to end the red lead before the final sun framing and instruction blocks. The TV world uses `signal-world/composition/SignalComposition.tsx` for independent white forms, merged red threads and sun; `VideoWork.tsx` shows sourced films as spatial surfaces. Only neighboring previews stream; distant decoders are released. `signalNavigation.ts` samples camera position and continuous yaw through 360°. `opening/useOpeningControls.ts` and `opening/useOpeningPointer.ts` own wheel/touch/keyboard input; `Opening.tsx` owns the index and History API project/About URLs. `TelevisionStatic` uses the same world camera for rendering, raycasting and 3D plaque placement, with fixed video-local label anchors after arrival. Dialogs suspend camera and world animations without unmounting the scene, while translucent film pages keep the TV signal behind readable content. `src/content/projects.ts` and `about.ts` contain content imported from the original portfolio; the human-readable portfolio checklist is in `docs/portfolio-content-inventory.md`. `ProjectDetail` and `AboutDetail` remain independent of WebGL. Lightweight `opening/timing.ts` preserves lazy loading of Three.js. Project credits are limited to the sourced content; real-device touch/performance has not been verified. These limits do not represent active tasks.

`signalLayout.ts` keeps the expanded world center, sun, close project viewing distance and media-block depths consistent across navigation and geometry. `VideoWork` maps film textures onto the front face of boxes with edge pixels stretched over the sides and a narrow central strip stretched over the back; exclusions account for their full depth and floating envelope. `signalFloat.ts` samples closed, bounded translation/tilt cycles shared by films and white forms; labels follow the same clock. The clock runs at priority -0.5, before object motion at 0 and compositing at 2, and freezes during loading, dialogs and reduced motion. `signalFlight.ts` integrates damped local camera movement from WASD/QE, with a bounded offset from the scroll path. Blur clears input; scroll/Locate restores the path. Input is released at the 11.4-second render handoff, rather than waiting for the automatic 2.2-second internal glide. The viewport contains only Work index and About buttons; `App` adds no header/footer and the canvas fills dynamic viewport height. One-finger touch travels and two-finger touch looks. Reduced motion starts directly inside the static signal. Project dialogs retain their own close controls and native media controls.

Test isolation stays enabled. Pure geometry/simulation tests use Node; React UI tests explicitly select jsdom with `@vitest-environment jsdom`. Four concurrent workers avoid competing DOM environments starving physics tests; timeouts and assertions are unchanged. See the [Vitest environment guide](https://vitest.dev/guide/environment.html) and [R3F performance guidance](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/advanced/scaling-performance) for the underlying APIs.

Wheel gestures are captured on window in the capture phase so moving labels and overlay controls cannot swallow navigation. Only vertical-dominant gestures select adjacent work stops; Shift+wheel is excluded. A 120 ms quiet period filters event bursts, but the first event after each completed journey bypasses it. No retargeting occurs in flight. Touch swipes and PageUp/PageDown share those stops. Travel uses a finite 2.8-second quintic ease with a 0.9-second orientation blend; position and input unlock finish together. During travel, look and free-flight input are suppressed; after the orientation blend, the world camera looks at the destination’s floating center. Arrival enables mouse inspection and WASD/QE flight; arrow look is used in free flight. At the title, every work and the final sun, `signalLook.ts` identifies stationary inspection and selects the shared +/-45-degree yaw/pitch ranges. WASD/QE input or a nonzero flight offset releases inspection immediately, enabling unlimited horizontal turns and vertical look up to +/-90 degrees. CameraMove rescales accumulated and damped look when leaving inspection to preserve the viewing angle. Mouse inspection and keyboard free look share the same predicate; touch clamps at stops without windup. Pointer, touch and keyboard look sensitivity are reduced by an additional 15%. Travel clears look offsets; dialogs preserve navigation state.

Before TV entry, `televisionRenderBounds.ts` projects the CRT corners into physical drawing-buffer pixels and applies a padded scissor rectangle to the offscreen target. This avoids shading the invisible part of the TV universe while retaining full target resolution, projection, grain and animation cadence. At entry, the same offscreen target is rendered without scissoring and composited across the full viewport, preserving the original CRT color and lighting treatment; render-target restoration is protected by finally. Regression tests cover corner coverage through entry in landscape and portrait, reduced shaded area at the initial pose, and restoration after a render failure. Browser visual review and device FPS measurements remain required to quantify the improvement.

The exterior uses a locally served NASA/JPL-Caltech/R. Hurt (IPAC) black-hole visualization on a distant curved cylinder. Source and usage links are in public/media/exterior/CREDITS.md. This is ordinary video with spatial occlusion and relative parallax, not stereoscopic or volumetric media. RealisticSpace uses an opaque, unblended material whose zero alpha marks pixels excluded from dither. OutputPass preserves alpha; the final dither shader resolves those pixels to opaque original full-resolution color before cell sampling, palette and tonal adjustments. Disabling dither makes the exterior write normal alpha. The room and CRT keep their existing render paths. The single muted decoder follows the opening clock, pauses with the scene, releases its source at TV entry; reduced motion never requests the video. Tests cover masking, playback, fallback and disposal. Browser visual review and device FPS remain unverified because Windows Computer Use could not confirm the Chrome URL.
